# Lead-Knee Flex at Address

## Metadata

- **Indicator key:** `lead_knee_flex_address_deg`
- **Label:** Lead-knee flex at address
- **Plain-language name:** How bent the lead knee is at setup
- **Swing phase:** Address
- **Unit:** Degrees
- **Handedness note:** “Lead knee” means the knee closer to the target. For a right-handed golfer, this is usually the left knee; for a left-handed golfer, it is usually the right knee.

## Measurement definition

Measures the angle of the lead knee at address to describe how bent or straight it appears in the setup position.

## Beginner explanation

Lead-knee flex describes how much the knee closer to the target is bent before the swing begins.

## Why it matters

This measurement helps describe the golfer’s lower-body setup position. It does not by itself determine balance, athletic posture, comfort, mobility, power, or whether the knee should be bent more or less.

## Evidence classification

- **Directly measured:** Lead-knee angle at address, in degrees.
- **Derived comparison:** Whether the value was inside, below, or above the tour reference range.
- **Not supported:** Balance, setup quality, mobility, strength, comfort, weight distribution, power, ball flight, or whether the golfer should change the knee angle.

## Approved glossary

- **Lead knee:** The knee closer to the target.
- **Knee flex:** How much the knee joint is bent.
- **Address:** The setup position immediately before the swing begins.
- **Tour reference range:** The typical spread of measured values seen across the professional swings used for comparison.

## Confidence and camera limitations

- This is a motion estimate derived from the available camera view.
- Knee-angle estimates can be affected by camera angle, leg visibility, clothing, stance orientation, body overlap, and pose-estimation quality.
- It should not be interpreted, compared, or revealed when the scorecard marks the measurement as low confidence.
- Handedness must be known before identifying a specific left or right knee as the lead knee.
- Use “measured knee” or the physical side when handedness is unknown.
- The tour range is a comparison reference, not an ideal target for every golfer.

## Allowed inferences

The LLM may:

- Describe the measured amount of lead-knee flex at address.
- State whether the reliable measurement was inside, below, or above the tour reference range.
- Say that a lower angle represents a more bent measured knee when the metric definition supports that interpretation.
- Say that a higher angle represents a straighter measured knee.
- Compare reliable setup measurements between two swings.
- Explain lead knee and knee flex in beginner-friendly language.
- State when the available measurement is not reliable enough to assess.
- Use the physical side instead of “lead knee” when handedness is unknown.

## Prohibited inferences

The LLM must not:

- Assume which physical knee is the lead knee unless handedness is available.
- Say the golfer was too bent, too straight, not athletic enough, or in the correct setup solely from the reference comparison.
- Describe the setup as correct, incorrect, good, bad, ideal, balanced, athletic, or faulty solely from this metric.
- Claim that the knee position caused poor contact, a slice, a hook, loss of distance, instability, or another shot result.
- Infer weight distribution, pressure under the feet, hip position, spine angle, stance width, or overall balance from this metric alone.
- Diagnose mobility, flexibility, strength, knee problems, or injury.
- Recommend a target angle, setup change, drill, or correction.
- Treat the tour reference range as the correct setup for every golfer.
- Interpret or reveal a value marked as low confidence.
