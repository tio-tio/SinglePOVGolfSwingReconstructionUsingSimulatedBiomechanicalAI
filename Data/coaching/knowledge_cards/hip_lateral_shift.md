# Hip Shift Toward the Target

## Metadata

- **Indicator key:** `hip_lateral_shift_pct`
- **Label:** Hip shift toward the target
- **Plain-language name:** How far the hips move sideways toward the target
- **Swing phase:** From setup to impact
- **Unit:** Percent of body height

## Measurement definition

Measures how far the hips moved sideways toward the target from setup to impact, expressed relative to the golfer’s body height.

## Beginner explanation

Hip shift describes the sideways movement of the pelvis toward the target during the swing.

## Why it matters

This measurement helps describe how the lower body changed position from setup to impact. It does not directly measure weight distribution, ground pressure, balance, or the cause of the movement.

## Evidence classification

- **Directly measured:** Sideways hip movement toward the target from setup to impact, relative to body height.
- **Derived comparison:** Whether the value was inside, below, or above the tour reference range.
- **Not supported:** Actual weight transfer, balance, ground-force use, power production, sequencing, ball flight, contact quality, or whether the golfer should move more or less.

## Approved glossary

- **Hip shift:** Sideways movement of the pelvis during the swing.
- **Toward the target:** Movement in the direction the golfer intends to hit the ball.
- **Percent of body height:** A body-size-adjusted way of expressing movement distance.
- **Tour reference range:** The typical spread of measured values seen across the professional swings used for comparison.

## Confidence and camera limitations

- This is a motion estimate derived from the available camera view.
- It describes pelvis movement, not actual weight distribution or pressure under the feet.
- It should not be interpreted or compared when the scorecard marks the measurement as low confidence.
- A single-camera estimate may be affected by camera angle, body visibility, pose-estimation quality, frame selection, and impact detection.
- The direction “toward the target” depends on the swing orientation being identified correctly.
- The tour range is a comparison reference, not an ideal target for every golfer.

## Allowed inferences

The LLM may:

- Describe the measured amount of lateral hip movement.
- State whether the measurement was inside, below, or above the tour reference range.
- Compare reliable hip-shift measurements from two swings.
- Explain the measurement in beginner-friendly language.
- Describe the movement neutrally.
- State that the metric is normalized relative to body height.
- State when the available measurement is not reliable enough to assess.

## Prohibited inferences

The LLM must not:

- Call the metric “weight transfer” as though body weight or ground pressure was directly measured.
- Say the golfer shifted “enough,” “not enough,” “too much,” or “too little.”
- Describe the movement as correct, incorrect, good, bad, ideal, or faulty solely from the reference comparison.
- Claim that the shift created or reduced power, speed, distance, balance, or consistency.
- Infer pressure under either foot or how the golfer used the ground.
- Claim that the hips slid, swayed, stalled, fired, or cleared unless the approved wording directly supports that description.
- Predict ball direction, contact quality, launch, slice, hook, fade, draw, or distance.
- Diagnose mobility, strength, posture, injury, or physical limitation.
- Recommend a drill, correction, target amount, or movement pattern.
- Treat the tour reference range as the correct target for every golfer.
- Interpret or reveal a value marked as low confidence.
