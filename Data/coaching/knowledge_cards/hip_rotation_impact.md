# Hip Rotation at Impact

## Metadata

- **Indicator key:** `hip_turn_impact_deg`
- **Label:** Hip rotation at impact
- **Plain-language name:** How open the hips are at impact
- **Swing phase:** Impact
- **Unit:** Degrees

## Measurement definition

Measures how far the hips rotated toward the target by the moment of impact.

## Beginner explanation

Hip rotation at impact describes how much the lower body had turned toward the target when the club reached the ball.

## Why it matters

This measurement helps describe the position of the lower body at impact. It does not by itself determine power, contact quality, ball flight, or swing efficiency.

## Evidence classification

- **Directly measured:** Hip rotation at the moment of impact, in degrees.
- **Derived comparison:** Whether the value was inside, below, or above the tour reference range.
- **Not supported:** Power production, sequencing quality, club path, clubface position, contact quality, ball flight, injury, or whether the golfer should rotate more or less.

## Approved glossary

- **Hip rotation at impact:** How far the hips have turned toward the target when the club reaches the ball.
- **Open hips:** Hips rotated to face more toward the target.
- **Impact:** The moment the club meets the ball.
- **Tour reference range:** The typical spread of measured values seen across the professional swings used for comparison.

## Comparison language

- The measured hip rotation at impact increased compared with the earlier swing.
- The measured hip rotation at impact decreased compared with the earlier swing.
- The measured hip rotation at impact was similar in both swings.
- The current measurement moved into the tour reference range.
- The current measurement moved outside the tour reference range.
- The current and earlier measurements were both inside the tour reference range.
- The current and earlier measurements were both outside the tour reference range.

## Confidence and camera limitations

- This is a motion estimate derived from the available camera view.
- It describes the measured hip position at impact but does not determine why the golfer moved this way.
- It should not be interpreted or compared when the scorecard marks the measurement as low confidence.
- A single-camera estimate may be affected by camera position, body visibility, pose-estimation quality, and the accuracy of impact detection.
- The tour range is a comparison reference, not an ideal target for every golfer.

## Allowed inferences

The LLM may:

- Describe the measured hip rotation at impact.
- State whether the measurement was inside, below, or above the tour reference range.
- Compare reliable impact-rotation measurements from two swings.
- Explain “open hips” in beginner-friendly language.
- Describe the lower-body position neutrally.
- State when the available measurement is not reliable enough to assess.

## Prohibited inferences

The LLM must not:

- Say the golfer was “open enough,” “not open enough,” “too open,” or “not open.”
- Describe the movement as correct, incorrect, good, bad, ideal, or faulty solely from the reference comparison.
- Claim that hip rotation created or reduced power, speed, distance, or consistency.
- Claim that the hips led or lagged the swing sequence unless a separate sequencing metric supports that conclusion.
- Infer club path, clubface angle, impact quality, launch direction, slice, hook, fade, draw, or another ball-flight result.
- Diagnose flexibility, mobility, strength, posture, or injury.
- Recommend a drill, correction, target angle, or amount of rotation.
- Treat the tour reference range as the correct target for every golfer.
- Interpret or reveal a value marked as low confidence.

### Below the reference range

### Above the reference range

### Comparison — decreased

Your measured hip rotation at impact decreased compared with the earlier swing. This shows how the two measured impact positions differed without judging the change as beneficial or harmful.

- “Your hips were not open enough.”
- “Your hips fired too early.”
