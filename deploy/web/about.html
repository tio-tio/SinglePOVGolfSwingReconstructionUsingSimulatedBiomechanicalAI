Swing feedback that needs data shouldn't need a lab

Objective, biomechanical feedback on a golf swing — not just "your shoulders open early" but an actual number for it — has always needed equipment most golfers never get access to.

What it takes today

Multi-camera launch monitors, marker suits, or an in-person session with a biomechanics coach. A single phone video, on its own, has no depth — so the 3D angles that actually matter (hip-shoulder separation, posture, weight shift) can't be measured from it directly.

What golfers actually have

A phone. That's it. Amateur players and coaches who want objective, repeatable feedback need a way to get real 3D biomechanical measurement out of ordinary 2D video — without buying anything or booking a lab session.

What MotionCaddie does

Upload one clip. Get a 3D replay, a tour-referenced scorecard, and a coach that only talks about what was measured.

⛳

3D from a single camera

A 2D pose model finds every joint in the frame, then a 3D lifting model reconstructs the depth a flat video can't show — no extra hardware.

📐

Tour-referenced indicators

Tempo, X-factor, posture, weight shift and more, computed at each swing event and scored against a tour-pro reference distribution.

💬

A coach that won't make things up

The explanation layer is only allowed to describe measured numbers. A grounding verifier checks every claim before it reaches the golfer.

Where the accuracy comes from

The event detector — the model that finds address, top, impact and follow-through — was the project's biggest technical win.

0.865
PCE@5 on held-out swing-event detection — beats the published GolfDB SwingNet baseline (~0.76)
15
Biomechanical indicators computed per swing and scored against tour-pro reference ranges
0%
Invented advice — every coaching claim is checked against the measured numbers before it's shown

Evaluated on 1,400 labeled clips from GolfDB across ~28,000 model × clip runs comparing pose backbones, 3D lifters, and event detectors. Full methodology and the complete leaderboard are in the repo's RESULTS.md.
